Undergraduate thesis · In progress

Risk-Based Wind Speed Mapping of Bangladesh

Developing reliable, spatially distributed wind-speed maps to support wind-resistant structural design and a better understanding of regional wind hazards.

Type
Undergraduate thesis
Institution
BUET, Civil Engineering
Field
Wind engineering
Status
Ongoing
WIND HAZARD · BANGLADESH Illustration only · result maps will be added as the research progresses

01Why it matters

Bangladesh is exposed to severe wind events, including tropical cyclones and squalls. Yet accurately estimating design wind speeds across the country remains challenging, because of gaps in meteorological data, differing exposure conditions from region to region, and the statistical uncertainty of extreme wind events.

This research builds a data-driven framework for evaluating wind hazard across Bangladesh, so that design wind speeds can better reflect where a structure actually stands.

02How the research works

01

Meteorological data analysis

  • Processing long-term wind observations from Bangladesh Meteorological Department (BMD) stations across the country.
  • Evaluating ERA5 reanalysis wind data against BMD observations, and bringing in cyclone and squall records.
02

Surface roughness & directional exposure

  • Studying the terrain and land cover around each station with GIS and satellite imagery.
  • Assessing exposure by direction using the provisions of ASCE 7-22 Section 26.7.
03

Extreme value analysis

  • Building annual maximum wind-speed datasets and applying extreme-value statistics, including Gumbel distribution-based analysis.
  • Estimating wind speeds for different mean recurrence intervals (MRIs), at conventional and risk-based design levels.
04

GIS-based wind hazard mapping

  • Interpolating station-based estimates into continuous wind-speed surfaces.
  • Producing maps that show how wind hazard varies across Bangladesh and which regions demand higher design wind speeds.

03The goal

More representative, geographically informed design wind-speed recommendations for Bangladesh's built environment.

The findings may also provide useful technical evidence for the future assessment and improvement of national wind-loading provisions, including the Bangladesh National Building Code (BNBC).

Data sources

  • BMD station observations
  • ERA5 atmospheric reanalysis
  • Cyclone & squall records
